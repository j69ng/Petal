Bhargo

Construction accounts — wages, material bills, and what a build is really costing
Software overview · Prepared for Bhargo Construction · 24 August 2026

Bhargo keeps a contractor's books and answers one question the books alone cannot: is this build being charged more than the last one, and by whom? This document sets out plainly what the software does, and — just as important — what it does not do.

What it does

Wages and attendance

Muster rollMark each day per worker — full day, half day, overtime hours. Day rates are stored against each day worked, so raising someone's rate later never changes what past work cost.
Wage sheetWhat each person earned, what they drew as advances, and what is still owed. Supports both daily wages and monthly salaries.
Labour costCost per square foot and man-days per square foot, per trade, so one build can be compared with another.

Materials and bills

Bill entryMaterial, quantity, unit, rate, freight, vendor, invoice number and date. Freight is kept separate so a low rate with heavy cartage cannot hide.
Usual pricesThe owner records what each material should cost. Every bill is checked against that figure as it is typed — before anyone signs for a delivery.
Rate bookWhat each supplier has quoted or been paid. When a rate is entered above the cheapest known, the software names the cheaper supplier, the percentage, and the money on that load.

Build-against-build comparison

Every cross-build figure holds three things still at once: the new house is a different size, prices drift on their own over the years, and the quantities differ. What remains is split into two numbers that always reconcile against the money:

  • Extra from rate — being charged a higher price per unit than last time, after normal market drift is allowed for.
  • Extra from quantity — more material billed than a house of that size needed last time. A fair rate on a padded quantity is still a problem, and this is the half most often missed.

The same arithmetic runs over labour, so a padded muster roll appears as extra man-days rather than an extra day rate.

Checks that need no comparison

On a single build's own bills, the software flags: the same bill entered twice, one vendor charging more than another for the same material, a rate that climbs mid-build, a single bill priced well above the rest, and large payments with no invoice number recorded.

Nothing counts until the owner confirms it

Entries made by site staff are held as claims. They stay out of every total, every wage balance and every comparison until the owner confirms them. Rejected entries stay on record with the reason attached, and editing a confirmed record sends it back for confirmation.

Built for the site

The five daily screens sit in a thumb bar on a phone; it can be added to a home screen and opens like an app. Accounts are per person, with owner and staff roles. All records live in a single file on the company's own machine or server.

What it does not do

Find suppliersIt cannot go out and discover local suppliers or their prices. No service publishes what the cement shop down the road charges today. It compares against rates you collect and rates you have already paid — which is why filling the rate book matters.
Prove dishonestyA flag is a question worth asking, not evidence. The figures are the company's own records; they cannot establish what a supplier did or intended.
Full accountingIt is not a bookkeeping package. No ledgers, trial balance, VAT or tax returns, no bank reconciliation, no statutory filing.
Bank or GST linksNo connection to banks, payment systems or tax portals. Nothing is filed on your behalf.
Read paper billsBills are typed in. There is no scanning or photographing of invoices.
Work offlineIt needs the machine or server it runs on to be reachable. There is no offline mode that syncs later.
Encrypt the fileAccounts protect the application, not the disk. Anyone with the server or a copy of the database file can read it. Keep the machine and its backups secure.
Estimate or designNo quantity take-off from drawings, no BOQ preparation, no scheduling, no design work.
Stop an overchargeIt tells you, with a number. Acting on it — the phone call, the argument, the changed order — is still a person's job.

What it needs

One of three arrangements, in order of cost and reach:

  • One office computer — free, used at that desk only.
  • The office network — free, reachable from any machine on the office wifi.
  • A server with HTTPS — roughly US$6–8 a month plus a domain name, reachable from any phone on any site.

Whichever is chosen, the database must sit on storage that survives a restart, and should be backed up nightly and copied off the machine.

On the company's records. All data stays in one file on the company's own machine or hosting account. No part of the software sends the company's figures to any third party. A copy of the file can be handed over at any time on request. Fault diagnostics record the error and the page it happened on — never bills, wages, rates or names.
